# The papers

- `1908.11170.pdf` — Abbott et al. (LVK), *A guide to LIGO–Virgo detector noise and
  extraction of transient gravitational-wave signals* (2020). The reference for everything
  in the lecture. Written to be reproduced; with sky localisation cut from our day, read the
  noise and matched-filtering sections and skip the rest.
- `1902.10341.pdf` — Venumadhav, Zackay, Roulet, Dai & Zaldarriaga, *A new search pipeline
  for compact binary mergers* (2019). The day's punchline: a search written independently of
  the collaboration's, finding events their catalogue does not contain. Read it for the
  *choices*, not the equations.
- `2312.06631.pdf` — Wadekar et al., *New black hole mergers in the LIGO–Virgo O3 data*
  (2023). More of them, with higher harmonics, and public data products you can download.

Read the second one. It is short, it is the argument for why today was worth your afternoon,
and one of its authors is standing at the front of the room.

The first one is a reference, not a reading assignment — but try this: after the session,
open it at the matched-filtering section and see how much you can now read without stopping.
That is the most honest measure of what the day was worth.

We do **not** reproduce 1908.11170 section by section, which breaks the day-1 pattern
deliberately. With localisation out of scope over half of it does not apply, and none of the
things the reproduction actually found — the template comparison, the L1 glitch, the bank
scaling — are in it. The reproduction target is our own claim instead: *single-detector
search, from strain to a false-alarm rate.*
