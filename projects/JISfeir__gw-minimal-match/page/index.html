Minimal match and SNR loss from imperfect templates

The web companion to the paper. The scientific claims and headline project values are shared with the PDF; this page adds supplementary diagnostics that do not fit within its page limit. Hover a highlighted value to see its caveat.

We quantify the SNR and volume loss caused by an imperfect gravitational-wave template in a deliberately restricted setting. Owen supplies the matched-filter geometry. The bank growth is inspired by Cokelaer, but our implementation replaces his explicit connector graph and adds its own boundary repair; it is therefore not a faithful reproduction of his placement algorithm.

1. Scope and definitions

This is a controlled bank study, not a reproduction of an observational search. It uses one detector, stationary Gaussian noise, the aLIGOZeroDetHighPower PSD from 20 to 1024 Hz, and non-spinning TaylorF2 waveforms terminated at fISCO. Component masses run from 5 to 50 solar masses. Results are separated into a main region with total mass at most 35 solar masses and an extended region above it, where an inspiral-only model visibly fails. Real searches additionally include spin dimensions, coincidence and rejection of non-Gaussian transients.

For normalized waveforms, the match maximizes the overlap over coalescence time and phase, and the mismatch is

μ = 1 − ℳ.

In noise-free data the recovered SNR fraction is exactly ℳ. A nominal minimal match MM bounds the design mismatch only if the bank actually covers the region. For sources uniform in Euclidean volume, the exact nominal worst-case loss at MM = 0.9700 is 1 − MM3 = 0.08733. The population averages below instead use injections uniform in component mass. They are not an astrophysical population model.

The symmetric mass ratio is

η = m1m2/(m1+m2)2 ≤ 1/4.

The upper bound follows from (m1−m2)2 ≥ 0; equality holds only for equal masses. Thus η = 1/4 is the physical equal-mass boundary, whereas μ denotes mismatch.

2. Method: Owen baseline and our placement

The metric we use

Following Owen, mismatch is expanded locally as μ ≈ gijΔλiΔλj. The implementation uses the analytic non-spinning 3.5PN TaylorF2 projected-phase metric in chirp-time coordinates (τ0,τ3) at 20 Hz. Constant phase and time are projected out; the frequency weight is proportional to |h(f)|2/Sn(f). PyCBC supplies the waveform and PSD. Derivatives use the base waveform's fixed TaylorF2 support ending at its fISCO.

A finite-difference calculation tests the quadratic predictor against a pre-registered relative-error limit of 0.10000. It passes at mismatch 0.03 but not at 0.05; only those discrete radii were tested. Results below MM = 0.97 are therefore metric extrapolations.

What comes from Cokelaer, and what we changed

Owen supplies the metric and target spacing, but not the complete finite-region growth rule used here. Square and hexagonal banks are grown generation by generation, with the local metric recomputed at each parent, following the idea of Cokelaer's cell-by-cell two-dimensional inspiral placement. Cells that cross η = 1/4 are projected back only after reproduction, also following Cokelaer's prescription.

Two rules are ours:

  • No explicit connectors. Cokelaer's mother–daughter and adjacent-daughter connector graph was replaced by a lattice-independent collision rule: a proposal is rejected if it lies within one half of the local spacing of an existing template, measured with the parent's metric. This makes both lattice implementations use the same duplicate rule, but does not preserve Cokelaer's topology and has not been shown equivalent to it.
  • Boundary repair. After growth, a strip within one covering radius of the region boundary is repaired greedily because the uncorrected banks left boundary holes.

Consequently, both the lattice-only and repaired template counts are properties of this implementation. They must not be described as Cokelaer counts.

3. Results

Waveform and discretisation losses

In the declared interpolation model, the TaylorF2 family plus bank loses 0.19320 of the volume in the main region, compared with 0.17580 for the interpolated family-only estimate. In the extended region the corresponding values are 0.51486 and 0.50779. The exact continuous-family loss would bound the discrete-bank loss from below, but the 28-point interpolation has no demonstrated one-sided error. Within this model, waveform-family error dominates bank discretisation at high mass.

Uniform injections do not certify strict covering. Adaptive optimisation reaches mismatch 0.0322 in the main hexagonal bank and 0.0362 in the extended hexagonal bank, both above the target. These are counterexamples to strict covering, not certified global maxima. The square banks survive the same search.

The measured hexagonal-to-square ratios at the headline minimal match are 0.7796 in the main region and 0.6885 in the extended region. The constant-metric, boundary-free value is 0.7698. Across the comparable banks in Cokelaer's Tables I–II, the recomputed range is 0.6605–0.7866 and the mean is 0.7191. That range is descriptive, not an uncertainty interval. Because our collision and boundary rules differ, this comparison neither validates nor refutes Cokelaer's implementation.

Boundary sensitivity is expected: 0.79900 of main-region injections and 0.93833 of extended-region injections lie within one covering radius of an edge. The count ratio consequently varies with region, minimal match and boundary treatment; it is not a universal lattice constant here.

The population-average losses lie below the exact nominal worst case because typical injections do not occupy a cell's worst point. This comparison applies only to the stated uniform component-mass measure.

Threshold and effective volume

Counting every template and time sample as independent is a conservative upper bound for the finite correlated Gaussian grid, but it is loose. Fitting simulated-noise maxima gives 4.063 × 105 equivalent independent trials per second for the main hexagonal MM = 0.97 bank.

Within each fixed region and lattice, fits of Neff ∝ Ntemplatesα give α = 0.1568, 0.1500, 0.1516 and 0.2214. Pooling unlike families gives 1.2922 instead, demonstrating that bank size by itself does not define the trials factor.

Calibration limit. The fitted effective count varies across the observed tail and is calibrated where the simulated maxima occur, then extrapolated to a substantially higher detection threshold. It is an empirical model, not a measured false-alarm rate at the operating point.

For the relative sensitivity proxy Veff = ⟨ℳ3⟩/ρ*3, the main hexagonal value changes by -1.87 % under naive counting and by 1.76 % under the fitted trials model. Even the sign therefore depends on the trials model.

The calibrated curves are nearly flat and do not resolve a robust optimum. They are shown without precision-style uncertainty bars because uncertainty in the effective-trials calibration and its tail extrapolation has not been quantified; an injection-only bar would be misleadingly incomplete.

4. Limitations

  • The delivered banks were not rechecked template by template with waveform overlaps; covering is evaluated with the quadratic metric.
  • The connector implementation, globally anchored metric sensitivity, discard-versus-project sensitivity and SVD cross-check were not completed.
  • The IMRPhenomD comparison approximately composes continuous-family and discretisation effects.
  • The effective-trials calibration does not reach the detection tail.

5. Conclusion: answers to the two questions

How much SNR is lost?

If the nearest template has match ℳ, then in noise-free data the recovered SNR is ρrec = ℳρopt. The fractional SNR loss is therefore exactly the mismatch, μ = 1 − ℳ. In the banks we actually built at nominal MM = 0.97, adaptive searches found losses of at least 0.0322 in the main hexagonal bank and 0.0362 in the extended hexagonal bank, both worse than the nominal 0.03. The square banks survived the same search, although that is not a proof of strict covering.

The metric-predicted population-average volume losses were 0.02088 (hexagonal) versus 0.01534 (square) in the main region, and 0.01285 versus 0.00945 in the extended region. Against IMRPhenomD signals, the approximate total loss for the hexagonal bank instead rises from 0.19320 in main to 0.51486 in extended; most of that is already present in the continuous TaylorF2 family (0.17580 and 0.50779). Thus the main/extended contrast is mainly a waveform-model result, not a bank-spacing result.

How dense must the bank be?

At MM = 0.97, our main bank contains 1772 hexagonal templates versus 2273 square templates; the extended bank contains 336 versus 488. The corresponding hexagonal-to-square count ratios are 0.7796 and 0.6885: hexagonal placement uses fewer templates in both regions, but by different amounts.

At fixed nominal minimal match, the delivered square banks trade a larger template count for lower population-average discretisation loss and survived the adaptive covering search. The hexagonal banks use fewer templates, but the implementations tested here contain small covering holes. Densifying the hexagonal placement reduces its loss, so these results do not establish that one lattice is universally better; the preferred choice depends on whether the objective is template count, average loss, strict coverage, or effective detection volume.

This comparison is especially sensitive to finite boundaries: 0.79900 of main and 0.93833 of extended injections lie within one covering radius of an edge. The square banks surviving our search is not a proof of strict coverage, and no robust optimal minimal match is resolved because the small Veff trend changes sign with the trials model.
