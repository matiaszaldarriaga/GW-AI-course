Chameleon bigravity and spin-2 dark matter

Final project, AI-assisted research (Claude Code + Codex) · Guadalupe Ahumada Acuña, DF–FCEN–UBA · September 2026

Can the massless gravitons of the metric \(g\) mix with those of a second dynamical metric \(f\) and produce massive spin-2 particles that behave as dark matter? I took my own bigravity calculation and used two agents to extend it to the chameleon case, \(\beta_n\to\beta_n(\phi)\), with a general relative lapse \(c(t)\) and background ratio \(\xi(t)\). For every result I recorded where it comes from and checked it along a path that shares no code with the one that produced it.

The model

\[ S=\frac{M_g^2}{2}\!\int\!\sqrt{-g}R[g]+\frac{M_f^2}{2}\!\int\!\sqrt{-f}R[f]+M_g^2m^2\!\int\!\sqrt{-g}\sum_{n=0}^{4}\beta_n(\phi)\,e_n\!\big(\sqrt{g^{-1}f}\big)-\tfrac12\!\int\!\sqrt{-g}(\partial\phi)^2+S_m\!\left[A^2(\phi)g\right] \]

The backgrounds are \(ds_g^2=-dt^2+a^2d\vec x^2\) and \(ds_f^2=\xi^2(t)\,[-c^2(t)dt^2+a^2d\vec x^2]\). My starting notes cover standard bigravity with \(c=1\) and constant \(\xi\). The goal is the stress-energy tensor at second order in perturbations.

Constraint. With \(\beta_n(\phi)\), the branch dichotomy \(J(H-\xi H_f)=0\) of standard bigravity is replaced by an inhomogeneous constraint. The Hubble-rate mismatch is driven by the rolling chameleon, not by matter:

\[ J\,(H-\xi H_f)=-\tfrac13\,\dot\phi\,\big(\beta_1'+3\beta_2'\xi+3\beta_3'\xi^2+\beta_4'\xi^3\big),\qquad c-1=\frac{\mathcal P-\Delta\,\xi H_f}{\widetilde W} \]

Tensor sector for general \(c(t)\), \(\xi(t)\). The \(f\)-graviton propagates on the light cone of \(f\), and the mass terms close on \(h-\gamma\):

\[ \ddot\gamma+\Big(3H+2\tfrac{\dot\xi}{\xi}-\tfrac{\dot c}{c}\Big)\dot\gamma+c^2\tfrac{k^2}{a^2}\gamma+m_\gamma^2(\gamma-h)=0,\qquad m_{\rm M}^2=m_h^2+m_\gamma^2=-\frac{m^2Z\,(c+\kappa\xi^2)}{\kappa\xi} \]

For \(c\neq1\) the gradient matrix \(\mathrm{diag}(1,c^2)\) does not commute with the mass matrix. So no constant basis decouples \(\mathcal G\) and \(\mathcal M\): there is a mixing channel \(\propto k^2(c^2-1)\) on top of the one driven by \(\dot\theta\). This is the starting point of the second-order calculation.

What I learned about the agents

  • Cross-auditing helps but does not converge by itself. Every fix brings new claims.
  • Copying instead of deriving: one of Claude's notebooks loads formulas from the paper and then "checks" against them.
  • Numerics where they do not belong: checks on a single numerical curve; a plot of two trivial straight lines.
  • Invented references in the derivation guide.
  • Silent failures: a patch by Codex failed its own checks and still wrote the file.
  • What worked: giving the agents my own code as the model; A/B with the same prompt; exact zeros from a clean kernel plus negative controls; re-deriving the key results along a path that shares no code; running the whole notebook from scratch, which exposed three latent bugs.

Environment: Python 3.12 (sympy 1.12, numpy, scipy, mpmath); Mathematica 12.1.1 for the two component derivations, with no xAct needed. The notebooks use xAct (xTensor 1.2.0, xPert 1.0.6, xPand 0.4.3).
