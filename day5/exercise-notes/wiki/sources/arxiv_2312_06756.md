# Where are NANOGrav's Big Black Holes?

**Source:** `references/2312.06756/` (arxiv 2312.06756)
**Bib key:** `SatoPolito:2023big` → see [[../bibliography]]
**Type:** arxiv paper (population model used by this project)
**Authors:** Gabriela Sato-Polito, Matias Zaldarriaga, Eliot Quataert (IAS + Princeton)
**Ingested:** 2026-04-13

## Summary

The measured amplitude of the nanohertz GWB from all PTA collaborations exceeds
theoretical predictions from the local SMBH mass function by 2–4.5 sigma. This
paper shows the discrepancy cannot be resolved by increasing the SMBH merger
rate: even with an infinite cascade of instantaneous equal-mass mergers, the
characteristic strain $h_c$ can grow by at most a factor of 1.64 (eq. for
$h^2_c|_{N\to\infty}$ as a convergent series). The resolution must therefore
lie in the mass function itself. Preserving the Soltan-argument estimate of
$\rho_{\rm BH}$ forces the conclusion that the SGWB is dominated by SMBHs of
$M^{\rm GW}_{\rm peak} \sim 3\times 10^{10}\,M_\odot$ — roughly 10 times higher
than naive predictions — corresponding to an undetected population of order 10
such objects within 100 Mpc.

## Key results

- **Amplitude discrepancy:** predicted $h_c$ from local scaling relations is
  2–4.5 sigma below PTA measurements for all combinations of VDF and $M-\sigma$
  relation tested (B10, B11, T20 VDFs; KH13, MM13 scaling relations).
- **Merger-rate upper bound:** infinite instantaneous equal-mass mergers give a
  convergent series boost of $\sum_{N=1}^\infty 2^{-2(N-1)/3} \approx 2.7$ in
  $h_c^2$, i.e. $h_c \to 1.64\,h_{c,\rm single}$. This is still below observed.
- **Required peak mass:** fixing $\rho_{\rm BH}$ to Soltan value implies
  $M^{\rm GW}_{\rm peak} \sim 3\times 10^{10}\,M_\odot$, approximately 10x
  larger than the fiducial $\sim 3\times 10^9\,M_\odot$.
- **Missing BHs prediction:** ~10 SMBHs of $3\times 10^{10}\,M_\odot$ or ~1 of
  $10^{11}\,M_\odot$ within 100 Mpc should exist but have not been found. Masses
  above ~few $\times 10^{10}\,M_\odot$ are further disfavored by PTA individual-
  source upper limits.
- **50% of $h_c$ from 5% tail:** even at fiducial $\varepsilon_0 = 0.38$, half
  the characteristic strain comes from black holes in the 5% tail of the
  $M-\sigma$ scatter distribution.

## Population model details

These are the parameters inherited directly by this project:

**Fiducial VDF (Bernardi et al. 2010, B10):**
$\phi_* = (2.61\pm0.16)\times 10^{-2}$ Mpc$^{-3}$,
$\sigma_* = 159.6\pm1.5$ km s$^{-1}$, $\alpha = 0.41\pm0.02$,
$\beta = 2.59\pm0.04$.

**Fiducial $M-\sigma$ relation (McConnell & Ma 2013, MM13):**
$\log_{10}M_{\rm BH} = a_\bullet + b_\bullet \log_{10}(\sigma/200\,\text{km s}^{-1})$
with $a_\bullet = 8.32\pm0.05$, $b_\bullet = 5.64\pm0.32$.

**Intrinsic scatter $\varepsilon_0$:**
- Fiducial: $\varepsilon_0 = 0.38$ (MM13 intrinsic; observed residuals give 0.43
  including measurement error). This is the value cited as the fiducial in
  `astrophysical_model.tex:22`.
- Needed to match PTA $h_c$ exactly while preserving $\rho_{\rm BH}$: $\varepsilon_0 = 0.84$
  ($x_1$ solution, $\sigma_* = 123$ km s$^{-1}$); within 90% CI: $\varepsilon_0 = 0.62$
  ($x_2$ solution, $\sigma_* \approx 150$ km s$^{-1}$). Both in strong tension with
  observations.
- This project's three scenarios (low/fiducial/high) use $\varepsilon \in \{0.20, 0.38, 0.66\}$
  (see `astrophysical_model.tex:19-23`); the figure $\varepsilon_0 = 0.62$ from this paper
  directly motivates the high-scatter case.

**$M^{\rm GW}_{\rm peak}$ (analytic, no scatter):**
$M^{\rm GW}_{\rm peak} = M_* [(\alpha + \tfrac{5}{3}b_\bullet)/\beta]^{b_\bullet/\beta}$;
shifts as $\log M_{\rm peak} \propto \varepsilon_0^2$ with scatter.
Fiducial value at $\varepsilon_0=0.38$: $\log_{10}(M_{\rm peak}/M_\odot)\approx 9.5$
(matches entry in `concepts/SMBH_population_model.md`).

**Merger redshift and mass-ratio distributions:**
$p_z(z) = z^\gamma e^{-z/z_*}$ with $\gamma=0.5$, $z_*=0.3$;
$p_q(q) = q^\delta$ with $\delta=-1$, $q_{\rm min}=0.1$.
Gives $\langle(1+z)^{-1/3}\rangle = 0.9$ and $\langle\eta\rangle = 0.18$.

**Scatter boost formulas (analytic):**
$\rho_{\rm BH} = \rho_{\rm BH,0}\,e^{\varepsilon_0^2\log^2(10)/2}$;
$h_c^2 = h_{c,0}^2\,e^{(25/18)\varepsilon_0^2\log^2(10)}$.

## Relevance to the project's argument

This paper provides the population model the project runs its KL-hierarchy
argument through. Crucially it establishes that the high-$\varepsilon_0$ regime
(large scatter, heavy-tailed mass function) is astrophysically motivated by the
PTA amplitude excess. This motivates examining $\varepsilon = 0.66$ as the
scenario where anisotropy is strongest — and where $C_\ell$-based searches are
most severely outperformed by coherent source fitting.

## Caveats / open questions

- The paper uses $\varepsilon_0$ for intrinsic $M-\sigma$ scatter; this project
  uses $\varepsilon$ (no subscript). Treat as identical parameters.
- The "fiducial" $\varepsilon_0 = 0.38$ is the intrinsic scatter from MM13.
  The observed residual scatter (including measurement error) is 0.43; the paper
  is explicit about this distinction (caption of Fig. for residuals).
- $M^{\rm GW}_{\rm peak}$ values for the three project scenarios ($\varepsilon \in
  \{0.20, 0.38, 0.66\}$) are stated in `astrophysical_model.tex` but not derived
  from this paper's analytic formula directly — they come from numerical integration
  of the power kernel. The analytic formula above applies strictly only in the
  no-scatter limit.
- No journal reference is known. The bibliography entry is correctly `@misc` with
  `eprint` only.
