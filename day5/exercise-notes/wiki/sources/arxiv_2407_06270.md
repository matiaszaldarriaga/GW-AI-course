# Spectral Variance in a Stochastic Gravitational-wave Background from a Binary Population

**Source:** `references/2407.06270/` (arxiv 2407.06270)
**Bib key:** `LambTaylor:2024` → see [[../bibliography]]
**Type:** arxiv paper
**Ingested:** 2026-04-13

## Summary

Lamb & Taylor (ApJL 971 L10, 2024) derive the first analytic frequency-scaling
relations for the variance, skewness, and kurtosis of the GWB spectrum across
realizations of a finite SMBHB population. The mean spectrum (Phinney 2001) is
well known; this paper extends that framework to higher moments. For a binary
frequency evolution df/dt ∝ f^lambda, the key scalings for Omega_GW(f) are:
mean ∝ f^(13/3 - lambda), variance ∝ f^(26/3 - lambda), skewness ∝ f^(lambda/2),
kurtosis ∝ f^lambda. For GW-hardened circular binaries (lambda = 11/3) these
become f^(2/3), f^5, f^(11/6), f^(11/3). The analytic results are validated
against 10^7 Monte Carlo realizations of a Schechter-based SMBHB population
(five models drawn from Sato-Polito & Kamionkowski 2023). A key clean observable:
the variance-to-mean ratio Var[Omega]/mean[Omega] scales independently of lambda,
providing a direct probe of Poissonian source statistics without needing to specify
binary-hardening dynamics.

## Key results

- **Variance formula** (Eq. 10): Var[Omega_GW(f)] = (4 pi^4 / 9 H_0^4) * (f^4 /
  Delta_ln_f) * integral d_theta <N(f,theta)> h^4(f,theta). The variance receives
  a 1/Delta_ln_f factor from the finite bin width; it is the integral of h^4 (not
  h^2) weighted by the expected source count.
- **Power-law scalings** (Table 1): For df/dt ∝ f^lambda,
  Var[Omega_GW] ∝ f^(26/3 - lambda), Var[h_c^2] ∝ f^(14/3 - lambda),
  Var[S_h] ∝ f^(8/3 - lambda), Var[S_delta_t] ∝ f^(-4/3 - lambda).
- **Lambda-free ratio**: Var[Omega] / mean[Omega] is lambda-independent;
  its frequency scaling cancels. This is highlighted as a clean diagnostic.
- **Skewness and kurtosis** (Eqs. 12-13) are always positive (right-skewed,
  heavy-tailed). Skew ∝ f^(lambda/2), Kurt ∝ f^lambda. Kurt / Skew^2 is
  frequency-independent, another Poissonian signature.
- **Characteristic-mass dependence**: higher M_* (more high-mass binaries)
  dramatically increases variance; for log(M_*/Msun) = 9, the mean spectrum
  lies outside the 68% CI at high frequencies, meaning the median strongly
  departs from the mean (few dominant sources per bin).
- **Consistency check**: results agree with Mingarelli et al. (2013) Eq. 29,
  which gives sigma(Omega)/Omega ∝ f^(11/6) for GW-hardened binaries.

## Concepts touched

- shot_noise (variance arises from Poisson sampling of discrete binaries per bin)
- SMBH_population_model (five Schechter-based models from SK23 alias)
- source_background_degeneracy (finite population produces non-power-law
  realizations; mean vs. median distinction)
- N_eff (implicitly: variance increases when N_eff is small, i.e., bin
  occupation number O(1))
- brightest_source_fraction (high M_* regime approaches O(1) source per bin)

## Relevance to the project's argument

This paper motivates step 2 of the project's observational hierarchy
(discussion.tex, "Frequency-to-frequency scatter"): even within the isotropic
spectrum, bin-to-bin variance is an informative probe of the source population.
It is complementary to anisotropy searches and does not require any angular
measurement. The analytic Var ∝ f^5 (for lambda = 11/3) and the lambda-free
ratio observable directly support the project's claim that spectral statistics
carry more information about finite-population effects than C_ell compression.
Unlike C_ell which scales as p^4 in KL divergence, spectral variance is a
direct moment of the one-point distribution of h_c^2(f) and does not involve
the angular response at all.

## Caveats / open questions

- The analytic variance formula (Eq. 10) assumes sources in different sky cells
  are uncorrelated (Eq. 8). This is exact for the Poisson model but ignores GW
  signal interference. The authors note that interference will contribute
  additional variance and higher moments, deferred to future work.
- All numerical results assume circular binaries with lambda = 11/3 (GW
  hardening only). Non-circular or environmentally hardened binaries have
  different lambda and different moment scalings.
- The letter considers only circular binary populations; extension to eccentric
  orbits is mentioned but not done.
- Skewness and kurtosis estimates become numerically unstable at high frequency
  for models with large M_* due to insufficient sampling of the tail (need
  >= 10^8 realizations, not yet feasible in this paper).
- The Var[h_c(f)] leading-order approximation (∝ f^(7/3)) breaks down for
  models with large strain outliers at high frequencies.
