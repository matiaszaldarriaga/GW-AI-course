# Mapping the Gravitational-wave Sky with LISA: A Bayesian Spherical Harmonic Approach

**Source:** `references/2103.00826/` (arxiv 2103.00826)
**Bib key:** `Banagiri:2021lisa` — see [[../bibliography]]
**Type:** arxiv paper
**Ingested:** 2026-04-13

## Summary

Banagiri et al. (2021) present a Bayesian algorithm for mapping the angular power
distribution of a stochastic gravitational-wave confusion noise (SGCN) with LISA
using a spherical harmonic basis. The central technical contribution is the
Clebsch-Gordan (CG) decomposition for enforcing positivity: instead of expanding
the GW power sky $\mathcal{P}(\hat n)$ directly in spherical harmonics $a_{LM}$,
one writes $\mathcal{P} = \mathcal{S}^2$ where $\mathcal{S} = \sum_{LM} b_{LM}
Y_{LM}$ is real, so $\mathcal{P} \ge 0$ everywhere by construction. The $a_{LM}$
coefficients are then quadratic in $b_{LM}$ via the CG coupling tensor
$\beta^{LM}_{\ell m,\ell' m'}$. The paper derives the truncation rule
$\ell_{\max}^b = \ell_{\max}^a / 2$ directly from the CG selection rule
$L_{\max} = \ell + \ell'$, establishes the degeneracy-breaking normalization
$b_{00} = 1$, and validates the method with LISA simulations including the galactic
DWD foreground. This is the foundational source for the sqrt-SH basis that
NANOGrav adopted in their 15yr anisotropy analysis, and therefore the paper whose
implementation choices this project argues produced a prior masquerading as a data
constraint.

## Key results

- **Positivity via squaring:** $\mathcal{P}(\hat n) = [\sum_{LM} b_{LM} Y_{LM}(\hat n)]^2$
  is the correct parameterization. The $b_{LM}$ are unconstrained real numbers (for $M=0$)
  or amplitude+phase pairs (for $M \ne 0$), making Bayesian sampling well-posed without
  any numerical positivity check.

- **CG conversion formula:** $a_{L,M} = \sum_{\ell m}\sum_{\ell' m'} b_{\ell m}
  b_{\ell' m'} \beta^{LM}_{\ell m, \ell' m'}$, where $\beta$ is built from standard
  CG coefficients (Eq. 3.7 in the paper).

- **Truncation rule:** Because the CG rule gives $L_{\max} = \ell + \ell'$, holding
  $a_{LM}$ up to $\ell_{\max}^a$ requires $b_{LM}$ only up to
  $\ell_{\max}^b = \ell_{\max}^a / 2$ (assuming $\ell_{\max}^a$ even). This is
  stated explicitly in Sec. 3 (paragraph after Eq. 3.7).

- **Degeneracy breaking:** $\mathcal{P}$ is invariant under $\{b_{LM}\} \to \{-b_{LM}\}$
  and under global rescaling; both are broken by fixing $b_{00} = 1$.

- **Priors used in validation simulations:** $|b_{LM}| \sim U[0, 3]$ with uniform
  phase for $M \ne 0$; $b_{L0} \sim U[-3, 3]$. These are the paper's own validation
  priors. NANOGrav 15yr used a wider range $U[0, 50]$ / $U[-50, 50]$, which does not
  affect $C_\ell / C_0$ (amplitude cancels in the ratio) but was their implementation
  choice.

- **Prior sky support:** Fig. 2 shows the 2.5–97.5 percentile of the prior
  sky distribution spans roughly two orders of magnitude above and below a uniform
  distribution for $\ell_{\max}^a = 4$, $n_{\rm side} = 6$.

- **LISA validation:** End-to-end recovery of an injected anisotropic distribution
  achieves coherence $0.956^{+0.032}_{-0.064}$ (median, 95% CI) for one year of data
  at SNR $\approx 149$. Two-month run (SNR $\approx 59$) shows parity bimodalities
  because parity breaking by fixing $b_{00}=1$ is only approximate at low SNR.

- **Galactic DWD foreground:** Disk+bulge model recovered with coherence
  $0.924^{+0.018}_{-0.024}$ for one year of data using $\ell_{\max}^a = 4$.

## Relevance to the project's argument

This paper **supports** the project's argument by providing the exact mechanism being
critiqued. The project's key claim is that the NANOGrav 15yr "upper limits" on
$C_\ell/C_0$ are not data constraints but simply reproduce the prior of this
sqrt-SH basis. Banagiri et al. introduce the basis, the truncation rule, and the
prior structure. The project's Section `nanograv_prior.tex` shows that the
NANOGrav 15yr posterior matches the prior induced by $\ell_{\max}^b = 3$ at all
frequencies, which directly follows from this paper's construction. The paper does
**not** discuss how the prior on $C_\ell/C_0$ changes with $\ell_{\max}^b$ nor does
it address PTA applications; that generalization is the project's contribution.

## Caveats / open questions

- The paper's own validation priors use amplitude range $[0, 3]$ (Sec. 4.1), whereas
  NANOGrav used $[0, 50]$. The project states these give the same $C_\ell/C_0$
  prior (amplitude cancels in the ratio). This claim is plausible given the scale
  invariance shown in Sec. 3, but the project should verify it numerically if it
  has not already.
- The paper targets LISA, not PTAs. The CG basis and truncation rule are
  detector-agnostic, but the LISA response function and TDI channel structure are
  LISA-specific. No PTA-specific claims are made in this paper.
- The paper does not discuss how the prior on $C_\ell/C_0$ (as opposed to the
  pixel-domain prior) depends on $\ell_{\max}^b$. That analysis is entirely the
  project's own contribution.
