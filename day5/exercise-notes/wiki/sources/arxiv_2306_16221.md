# NANOGrav 15yr: Search for Anisotropy in the Gravitational-Wave Background

**Source:** `references/2306.16221/` (arxiv 2306.16221)
**Bib key:** `NANOGrav:2023tcn` → see [[../bibliography]]
**Type:** arxiv paper (the direct target of this project's critique)
**Ingested:** 2026-04-13

## Summary

Agazie et al. (NANOGrav, 2023) search for anisotropy in the 67-pulsar, 15-year NANOGrav
dataset using three methods: (1) Bayesian square-root spherical harmonic (sqrt-SH) basis,
(2) Bayesian linear spherical harmonic basis, and (3) frequentist optimal-statistic / radiometer
pixel analysis. No significant anisotropy is found. The headline result is a Bayesian 95%
upper limit $(C_{\ell>0}/C_0) < 20\%$. Comparison with holodeck-simulated SMBHB populations
conditioned on the 15yr GWB amplitude is presented as evidence that the dataset has
"sufficient sensitivity to probe a large fraction of the predicted level of anisotropy."

Published as ApJL 956 L3 (2023).

## Key results (anisotropy methods + upper limits)

**Methods used:**
- **Bayesian sqrt-SH basis** (`bumpy_bkgrnd` / Banagiri et al. 2021): $b_{00}=1$ fixed;
  $|b_{LM}|\sim U[0,50]$, uniform phase ($M\ne0$); $b_{L0}\sim U[-50,50]$.
  Analysis truncated at $\ell_{\rm max}^a = 6$ (S/N saturates there; diffraction limit is 8).
  This implies $\ell_{\rm max}^b = 3$ via the Clebsch-Gordan squaring rule.
- **Bayesian linear SH basis**: uniform priors $[-5,5]$ on $c_{lm}$, $c_{00}=\sqrt{4\pi}$ fixed.
- **Frequentist sqrt-SH + radiometer pixel basis**: NMOS cross-correlations, N_side=8 (768 pixels).

**Upper limits:**
- Bayesian 95% credible upper limit: $C_{\ell>0}/C_0 < 20\%$ (broadband, power-law template).
- Upper limits decrease monotonically with $\ell$ from $\ell=1$ to $\ell=6$ (by ~1 order of magnitude).
- Upper limits are uniform across the five lowest GW frequency bins.
- Frequentist anisotropic S/N $\approx 4$, corresponding to $p=0.05$ (not significant at $p<3\times10^{-3}$ threshold).
- Bayesian odds ratio for anisotropic vs isotropic model: $\approx 2$ (no significant detection).
- The paper's own caption (Fig. 1 caption) states: "the evolution of the upper limits as a function
  of spherical harmonic multipoles reflects the constraint from the prior condition that the power be
  positive on the sky as imposed by the square-root spherical harmonic basis."

**Hellinger distance analysis (Fig. 5 / `fig:bayesian_bin2`):**
The paper measures the Hellinger distance $H(P,Q)$ between the prior and posterior of $C_\ell/C_0$
for the frequency-resolved Bayesian analysis at each of the lowest five frequency bins. They find
$H\approx 0$ for all $\ell$ and all frequencies, and state: "there is no significant difference
between the posterior and prior distribution of the angular power for any of the lowest five
frequencies, implying that the data are consistent with isotropy in these bins." The Hellinger
distance is bounded $[0,1]$; $H=0$ means prior = posterior.

**$\ell_{\rm max}^a$ choice:**
Section 3 / Fig. 1: S/N saturates at $\ell_{\rm max}^a = 6$ (slightly below diffraction-limit
value of 8). This value is used throughout all analyses. With $\ell_{\rm max}^b = \ell_{\rm max}^a/2$,
the underlying sqrt-SH field is truncated at $L=3$.

**SMBHB population comparison:**
Using holodeck simulations conditioned on the 15yr GWB amplitude, the paper estimates
$C_\ell/C_0 \sim 4\times10^{-2}$ at $f=0.10\ {\rm yr}^{-1}$ rising to $\sim 2\times10^{-1}$
at $f=1.0\ {\rm yr}^{-1}$, with a 68% confidence interval spanning ~1 order of magnitude.
The upper limits are compared to these predictions, with the claim that they probe "a large
fraction" of the predicted range. No discussion of brightest-source fraction $p$ or KL hierarchy.
The paper notes: "Because the loudest single sources determine the level of anisotropy, one
can treat all but the 2000 loudest as perfectly isotropic."

## Relevance to the project's argument

**Which claims this project challenges:**

1. **The 20% upper limit is prior-driven, not data-driven.** The paper's own Hellinger distance
   analysis (their Fig. 5) shows $H\approx 0$ at all frequencies and multipoles: posterior $\equiv$
   prior. The paper acknowledges this in passing ("the analysis returns the priors"), but does not
   draw the conclusion that the "constraint" is therefore uninformative. The project's
   `nanograv_prior.tex` makes this explicit: the 95th percentile of $C_1/C_0$ from the
   $\ell_{\rm max}^b=3$ prior is $\approx 0.20$, matching their reported upper limit exactly.

2. **The $\ell_{\rm max}^a=6$ / $\ell_{\rm max}^b=3$ truncation.** The paper uses $\ell_{\rm max}^a=6$
   motivated by S/N saturation. Via the Clebsch-Gordan squaring rule the underlying source-sky
   is truncated at $L=3$. The project argues this truncation causes the prior on $C_\ell/C_0$ to be
   artificially tight: as $\ell_{\rm max}^b$ increases the 95th percentile of the prior drops by
   ~2 orders of magnitude. The choice $\ell_{\rm max}^b=3$ is conservative in this sense.

3. **$C_\ell$ compression is the wrong figure of merit.** The paper frames the anisotropy search
   entirely within $C_\ell/C_0$, with no discussion of the KL hierarchy
   (coherent $\propto p$ > incoherent covariance $\propto p^2$ > $C_\ell$ compression $\propto p^4$).
   No comparison to the sensitivity of coherent single-source search pipelines is made.

4. **Brightest source vs diffuse background.** The paper mentions that "the loudest single sources
   determine the level of anisotropy" and uses the 2000 loudest sources for its population model,
   but does not analyze the single-source contribution as a distinct signal component. The project's
   framework explicitly separates the brightest-source fraction $p$ from the diffuse background.

5. **The caption for Fig. 1 is self-incriminating.** The paper explicitly states that the
   $\ell$-dependent shape of the upper limits "reflects the constraint from the prior condition."
   This is direct evidence for the project's argument, in the paper itself.

## Caveats / open questions

- The paper uses real-valued spherical harmonics throughout (see Eq. 3, "real valued spherical
  harmonics"). The project wiki uses complex SH (Condon-Shortley). The $C_\ell$ ratios are
  convention-independent, but the $b_{LM}$ prior counts differ. Flag if $b_{LM}$ parameter
  counting matters for any downstream calculation.
- The paper does not state explicitly whether the Hellinger distances in Fig. 5 are computed
  for $C_\ell/C_0$ or for the raw $C_\ell$. Context implies $C_\ell/C_0$, consistent with their
  discussion, but this should be verified against the MAPS code if a precise statement is needed.
- The SMBHB population comparison uses $C_\ell^{(P)}$ of the holodeck sky maps (via HEALPix
  `anafast`), which is the source-power sky spectrum. This is the same convention as the project
  ($C_\ell \equiv C_\ell^{(P)}$). No conflict, but worth noting since the ORF analysis involves
  the pulsar-response map $C_\ell^{(b)}$.
- The paper does not define $p$ (brightest-source fraction) anywhere. The project's entire
  framework is built around $p$; this paper's null result is what the KL hierarchy explains.
