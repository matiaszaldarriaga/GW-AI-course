## Summary

Agazie et al. (NANOGrav, 2023), ApJL 951 L8: the 15-year, 67-pulsar dataset
shows evidence for a stochastic GWB with Hellings–Downs inter-pulsar
correlations. The GWB model is favored over an uncorrelated common
power-law-spectrum (CURN) model with Bayes factors of 200–1000; the
frequentist false-alarm probability of the observed HD correlation is
$p = 5\times10^{-5}$–$1.9\times10^{-4}$ ($\approx 3.5$–$4\sigma$).

## The $\rho_0 \approx 5$ number used in paper_v2 (which SNR variant it is)

paper_v2 anchors every detection statement to $\rho_0 \approx 5$. The exact
provenance, from Sec. 4 of the paper:

- It is the **noise-marginalized optimal-statistic signal-to-noise ratio for
  HD-correlated power**: "The noise-marginalized optimal statistic, which is
  constructed to be selectively sensitive to HD-correlated power, achieves a
  signal-to-noise ratio of ∼5" (Sec. 1); "We find S/Ns of 5±1 and 4±1 for
  CURN$_\gamma$ and CURN$_{13/3}$, respectively" (Sec. 4, Fig. 4).
- paper_v2 quotes the varied-$\gamma$ value, $5\pm1$, rounded to $\approx5$.
- It is a **total** over all pulsar pairs *and* all frequencies (quadrature).
  The paper gives **no per-frequency-bin HD S/N**; paper_v2's "of order
  $5/\sqrt{5}\approx2$ per bin" spreads the total over the ~5 informative
  low-frequency bins, an estimate made explicitly in paper_v2 Sec. V.A.
- It is NOT the (much larger) significance of the common red-noise process,
  and NOT the Bayes factor. Anisotropy lives in the cross-correlations, so
  the HD cross-correlation S/N is the relevant $\rho_0$ (paper_v2 Sec. V.A,
  point i).
