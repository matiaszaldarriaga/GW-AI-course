# Big Galaxies and Big Black Holes: Revised Local GSMF from MASSIVE and GWB Implications

**Source:** `references/2407.14595/` (arxiv 2407.14595)
**Bib key:** `Liepold:2024big` → see [[../bibliography]]
**Type:** arxiv paper — published ApJL 971 L29 (2024)
**Ingested:** 2026-04-13

## Summary

Liepold & Ma construct a revised $z=0$ galaxy stellar mass function (GSMF) by
combining the \citet{lejaetal2020} census for $M_* \la 10^{11.3} M_\odot$ with
new, volume-limited measurements from the MASSIVE survey at
$M_* \ga 10^{11.5} M_\odot$. MASSIVE stellar masses are derived via either
Schwarzschild orbit modeling or stellar population synthesis (SPS) with
bottom-heavy IMF; the two methods agree to within ~7%. Their GSMF has a
substantially higher amplitude at $M_* \ga 10^{11.5} M_\odot$ than all prior
SDSS-based studies (Bernardi 2013, D'Souza 2015, Moustakas 2013, Leja 2020).
The implied BHMF — obtained by convolving the GSMF with the McConnell & Ma 2013
$M_{\rm BH}$–$M_*$ relation — predicts a GWB amplitude at $f = 1$ yr$^{-1}$ of
$h_c = 2.0^{+0.7}_{-0.5} \times 10^{-15}$ (90% CI), consistent with PTA
detections. This is in contrast to the tension found when the galaxy velocity
dispersion function is used as the \mbh proxy; the authors argue that
$\sigma$ underpredicts $M_{\rm BH}$ in very massive galaxies because
dissipationless (gas-poor) mergers grow $M_*$ while leaving $\sigma$ unchanged.

## Key results

- **Revised high-mass GSMF**: new fiducial is a double-Schechter fit (Eq. 3),
  with amplitude 2–3x higher than Leja+2020 at $M_* \ga 10^{12} M_\odot$.
- **IMF correction**: MASSIVE galaxies have bottom-heavy IMF,
  $\alpha_{\rm IMF} = 1.84$ relative to Milky Way; prior SDSS studies assumed
  Chabrier/Kroupa. A simple $M_*$ shift by this factor approximately reconciles
  Bernardi+2013 and D'Souza+2015 with this work.
- **BHMF** (from $M_{\rm BH}$–$M_*$, McConnell & Ma 2013):
  predicts 1–14 SMBHs with $M_{\rm BH} \ga 10^{10} M_\odot$ within the
  MASSIVE survey volume, consistent with dynamically known systems.
- **GWB amplitude** (Eq. 6–7): $h_c(f\!=\!1\,{\rm yr}^{-1}) =
  2.0^{+0.7}_{-0.5} \times 10^{-15}$. Dominant contribution from
  $M_{\rm BH} \sim (1$–$3) \times 10^9 M_\odot$, not ultra-massive BHs.
  No need for a Sato-Polito+2023 ad-hoc $M_{\rm BH} \propto \sigma^{10.5}$
  high-mass tail.
- **$\sigma$-based BHMF tension**: using Bernardi+2010 VDF + McConnell & Ma
  $M_{\rm BH}$–$\sigma$ gives $h_c \approx 1.1 \times 10^{-15}$, low compared
  to PTAs — consistent with Sato-Polito+2023 tension finding.
- **SMBH mass density anomaly**: all $M_*$-based BHMFs give
  $\rho_{\rm BH} \sim 10^6 M_\odot\,{\rm Mpc}^{-3}$, a factor ~3–5 above
  quasar-based QLF estimates. Left as an open puzzle.

## Relevance to the project's argument

This paper supplies a high-end GSMF input that is more consistent with PTA
amplitudes than the velocity-dispersion-based approach. For the project's
population model, the Liepold & Ma GSMF implies a higher number density of
$M_{\rm BH} \sim 10^9 M_\odot$ binaries, which increases the expected GWB power
without invoking a tail of ultra-massive ($M_{\rm BH} \ga 10^{10} M_\odot$)
objects. This modestly raises the expected $N_{\rm eff}$ (more sources sharing
the power), pushing toward the background-dominated regime and reducing $p$
relative to the Sato-Polito+2023 high-scatter scenario. The paper does not
compute $p$, $N_{\rm eff}$, or $C_1/C_0$ directly; those quantities must be
propagated from the BHMF by the project's own population model.

## Caveats / open questions

- The paper uses a single-merger approximation (each SMBH experienced one
  merger) and separable $M$–$q$–$z$ distributions. The project's population
  model should clarify which merger rate prescription it inherits.
- No DOI is recorded in `references.bib`; eprint is present and sufficient.
- The SMBH mass density puzzle ($\rho_{\rm BH}$ from $M_*$-based BHMF exceeds
  QLF estimates by 3–5x) is unresolved; it does not directly affect $h_c$ or
  anisotropy predictions but may indicate systematic overestimate of $M_*$.
