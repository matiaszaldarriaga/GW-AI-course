## Summary

Taylor, van Haasteren, Sesana (2020) develop and compare four Bayesian sky-mapping
strategies for PTA anisotropy: (i) spherical-harmonic (SH) decomposition of the power
P(Omega), (ii) SH decomposition of the square-root of power P(Omega)^(1/2) — the
sqrt-SH method — (iii) discrete point sources optionally stacked atop an isotropic
background, and (iv) a disk model for extended hotspots. They test these on two
simulated skies: an artificial hotspot and a realistic SMBBH population drawn from
a population-synthesis catalog (Sesana 2013). The central empirical result is that
detecting power anisotropy from a realistic SMBBH sky is very hard: SNR > 25-28 is
required to reach Bayes factor 100:1, and the ORF of the realistic population sky
matches the isotropic ORF at the M = 0.98 level. The sqrt-SH method performs best
for the SMBBH sky. The point-source-plus-isotropic-background model is the most
physically transparent representation, correctly localizing the top-5 loudest
binaries once SNR > 35 or so.

## Key results

- Geometrical ORF match to isotropy: M = 0.98 for the realistic SMBBH population sky
  (vs M = 0.78 for an extreme hotspot). The signal correlations seen by a PTA are
  nearly identical to those of an isotropic background even when a few sources dominate.
- Anisotropy detection threshold: B = 100 reached at SNR ~ 28 (sqrt-SH, L_max = 6)
  and SNR ~ 32 (standard SH, l_max = 3) for the SMBBH sky. For an extreme hotspot,
  SNR ~ 10 suffices (consistent with Haris et al. 2019 cited in the text).
- Sqrt-SH basis: avoids the non-analytic physical prior on c_lm and improves sampling.
  A map restricted to l <= l_max in power requires L up to L_max in the root-power
  expansion (Appendix A derives this relation via Clebsch-Gordan coefficients). This
  is the result cited as the origin of the l_max^b = l_max^a / 2 truncation in the
  project's conventions.
- Point-source model: models each bright binary as a single lit pixel atop an isotropic
  background, with per-source amplitude. Disfavored below SNR ~ 25 due to Occam
  penalty; preferred by SNR ~ 35 for N_pts >= 2.
- Suboptimal-to-optimal SNR ratio: ~ 0.97 for the SMBBH sky (using an isotropic
  template vs the true sky). The isotropic template loses very little SNR for a
  realistic population.
- The paper explicitly notes that in some population realizations one source can
  overwhelmingly dominate — in that case point-source modeling is the preferred
  model — but this regime was NOT the focus of the study.

## Relevance to the project's argument

The paper is directly supportive of the project's narrative in several ways:

1. Quantifies how hard C_l-based anisotropy is to detect: the SMBBH population sky is
   98% matched to isotropy in ORF space, requiring very high SNR to distinguish.
   This is exactly the regime where the project argues C_l compression fails (p^4 KL
   scaling vs coherent p scaling).

2. Bright-source regime acknowledged but not fully explored: the paper notes (Conclusion
   point 3) that "sometimes a single binary can overwhelmingly dominate the entire strain
   budget" and that in that case the single-pixel model would be most preferred. This is
   precisely the p ~ 1 regime the project focuses on, and the paper declines to study it.

3. Sqrt-SH basis: the project's wiki/conventions.md already cites Banagiri et al.
   (2103.00826) as the reference for the sqrt-SH basis and the l_max^b = l_max^a / 2
   rule. The current paper independently derives the same basis and the same Clebsch-
   Gordan truncation one year earlier. This is a priority question; the project should
   be aware that Taylor et al. 2020 is an earlier independent source for the sqrt-SH
   method, even if it does not supersede Banagiri et al. for the specific context of
   the NANOGrav 15yr analysis.

4. The paper does NOT address the KL/Fisher hierarchy (coherent vs incoherent vs C_l)
   that is the project's central argument. It remains within the incoherent power
   framework throughout.

## Caveats / open questions

- Pre-dates 2023 GWB detections; uses a 100-pulsar idealized array. SNR thresholds
  (25-35) are not directly comparable to current NANOGrav 15yr sensitivity.
- Stays entirely within the incoherent-power framework; does not compare to coherent
  single-source searches and therefore does not test the KL hierarchy directly.
- M = 0.98 is specific to the Sesana 2013 population realization used; other
  realizations (especially p ~ 1) can be substantially more anisotropic.
- The Appendix A L_max / l_max Clebsch-Gordan relation should be verified against
  wiki/conventions.md (l_max^b = l_max^a / 2) before citing; notation direction
  (L vs l, ^b vs ^a) differs between the two papers.
