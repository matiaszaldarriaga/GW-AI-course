# Exploring the spectrum of stochastic gravitational-wave anisotropies with PTAs

**Source:** `references/2305.05690/` (arxiv 2305.05690)
**Bib key:** `SatoPolito:2024Kam` → see [[../bibliography]]
**Type:** arxiv paper
**Ingested:** 2026-04-13

## Summary

Sato-Polito & Kamionkowski (PRD 109, 123544, 2024) derive an analytical
model for the angular power spectrum $C_\ell(f)$ of the GWB intensity
produced by a Poisson-distributed population of inspiralling SMBHBs.
The central observation is that because each binary emits at a fixed
(twice orbital) frequency, the shot-noise anisotropy is strongly
frequency-dependent: $C_{\ell>0}/C_0 \propto f^{11/3}$ in the
GW-emission-dominated regime, so the anisotropy is largest at the
highest PTA frequencies and largely washed out when all bands are
combined. The authors parametrize five SMBHB mass-function models
(all normalized to the same characteristic strain) and show that
anisotropy measurements probe the second moment of the GW intensity
distribution $\sim\int dN\,h^4$, complementary to the isotropic
monopole which probes the first moment $\sim\int dN\,h^2$. They also
model the impact of additional binary-hardening mechanisms (stellar
scattering, circumbinary gas) and derive the expected circular and
linear polarization anisotropies from the distribution of binary
inclination angles.

## Key results

- **Shot-noise $C_\ell$ formula** (Eq. 9 of the paper):
  $C_{\ell>0}(f) = \left(\frac{f}{4\pi\Delta f}\right)^2
  \int d\vec\theta\,\frac{dN_{\Delta f}}{d\vec\theta}\,h^4(f,\vec\theta)$,
  with the monopole $C_0$ given by the square of the first moment.
  $C_\ell$ is white (flat in $\ell$) — pure shot noise.
- **Frequency scaling** (GW-driven limit):
  $C_\ell/C_0 \propto 1/[1+(f/f_*)^{-11/3}]$; rises as $f^{11/3}$
  at low $f$ and saturates at high $f$.
- **Dynamical effects enhance anisotropy**: stellar scattering
  ($\kappa=10/3$) or circumbinary-gas ($\kappa=7/3$) hardening
  reduces source counts and boosts $C_\ell/C_0$ below a bend
  frequency $f_b$, with spectrum modified by $[1+(f_b/f)^\kappa]^{-1}$.
- **Polarization content**: circular polarization anisotropy $\sim
  C_\ell^{(V)}/C_\ell^{(I)} = (92/105)/(284/315) \approx 0.97$ (order
  unity); linear polarization anisotropy $\sim (8/315)/(284/315)
  \approx 3\%$.
- **Mass-function degeneracy**: all five models have identical $h_c(f)$
  but differ in $\int dN\,h^4$; anisotropy breaks the degeneracy.
- Combining all frequencies into a single broadband $C_\ell$ measurement
  strongly suppresses the detectable signal; frequency-resolved searches
  are essential.

## Concepts touched

- `shot_noise` — primary mechanism; white $C_\ell$ from finite source counts
- `SMBH_population_model` — five-parameter mass function
  (Eq. 3/Middleton et al. 2015), varied over $\alpha$, $M_*$, $\beta$, $z_0$
- `Cl_over_C0` — main observable; frequency-dependent ratio; paper's
  $C_\ell$ is source-power sky $C_\ell^{(P)}$ (wiki convention default)
- `transfer_function` — not computed here; $C_\ell$ is of the GW-intensity
  sky, not pulsar residuals
- `coherent_vs_incoherent` — entirely incoherent regime; no coherent fitting

## Relevance to the project's argument

This paper occupies the "C_ell compression" tier of the KL hierarchy:
it computes the expected signal in the very statistic ($C_\ell/C_0$)
that the project argues is sub-optimal. Key points of contact:

1. The $f^{11/3}$ frequency scaling directly implies that the brightest-
   source fraction $p$ grows with frequency, reinforcing why the
   incoherent $C_\ell$ signal only becomes detectable at the highest PTA
   bins — precisely where a single loud source can dominate and coherent
   fitting overtakes $C_\ell$-compression by $p^{-2}$.
2. The "second moment vs first moment" framing ($h^4$ vs $h^2$) is
   consistent with the project's $p^2$ vs $p^4$ KL hierarchy: the
   $C_\ell$ signal scales as $\langle N^{-1}\rangle \propto p^2$ of the
   brightest source relative to $C_0$, so Fisher on $C_\ell$ goes as
   $p^4$.
3. The frequency-band averaging caveat is directly relevant: the project
   should emphasize that any broadband $C_\ell$ analysis is even less
   competitive than the already-disfavored frequency-resolved one.
4. Polarization prediction (3% linear, ~100% circular relative to
   intensity) is a secondary observable; the project does not currently
   address polarization.

## Caveats / open questions

- The paper's $C_\ell$ is explicitly the source-power sky (GW intensity
  map), consistent with wiki convention. The transfer function to
  pulsar-timing residuals is not derived here; that step is needed to
  compare with NANOGrav measurement outputs.
- The "all-band combined" $C_\ell/C_0$ shown vs Pol et al. 2022
  sensitivity threshold is described as "not apples-to-apples"; the
  exact SNR penalty for frequency-resolved vs broadband is not
  quantified.
- Eccentricity is explicitly left for future work; it would reduce
  anisotropy by broadening the source contribution per frequency bin.
- `Kumar_prep` (in-prep at submission) on linearly polarized SGWB
  detectability is cited; unclear if it was published.
