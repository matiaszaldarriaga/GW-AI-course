# Analytical Estimates of GWB Anisotropies from Shot Noise and LSS in PTAs

**Source:** `references/2602.16808/` (arxiv 2602.16808)
**Bib key:** `LinLidzMa:2026` → see [[../bibliography]]
**Type:** arxiv paper (second target of this project's critique)
**Ingested:** 2026-04-13

## Summary

Lin, Lidz & Ma (2026) derive analytical expressions for GWB anisotropy angular
power spectra in PTAs from two sources: (1) Poisson shot noise from the discrete
SMBHB population, and (2) large-scale structure (LSS) tracing. They calibrate both
against two empirical SMBHB merger-rate models (LM: Liepold & Ma; SZQ:
Sato-Polito, Zaldarriaga, Quataert) and compare predicted $C_\ell$ amplitudes
against the NANOGrav 15yr broadband upper bounds. Their central finding is that
predicted shot-noise $C_\ell$ values are comparable to or exceed the NANOGrav
bounds, and that LSS is 2–3 orders of magnitude smaller than shot noise.

Their notation: $C_{\ell,h^2}$ is the angular power spectrum of fractional
strain-amplitude fluctuations $\delta_{h^2} = (h^2 - \langle h^2\rangle)/\langle h^2\rangle$.
The NANOGrav convention differs: $(\tilde C_{\ell>0}/\tilde C_0)^{\rm NG} = (1/4\pi)\,C_{\ell,h^2}$.

## Key results (shot-noise formula + LSS contribution)

**Shot-noise power spectrum** (their Eq. 9, flat in $\ell$):
$$C_{\ell>0,h^2}^{\rm SN} = \frac{\langle h^4\rangle}{\langle h^2\rangle^2} \equiv \frac{4\pi}{N_{\rm eff}}$$

This is the $4\pi/N_{\rm eff}$ formula used in the `shot_noise` concept page.
It is $\ell$-independent and encodes the inverse participation ratio of source weights.
The numerator $\langle h^4\rangle$ involves an $M_{\rm BH}^5$-weighted integral over
the mass function (their Eq. 18), making shot noise highly sensitive to the
high-mass tail of the SMBH population.

**Frequency scaling** (their Eq. 12):
$$C_{\ell>0,h^2}^{\rm SN} \propto f^{8/3}$$
for GW-driven circular orbits; more generally $\propto f^\beta$ where $\beta = d\ln f_r/dt_r$
encodes the binary residence time. Gas/stellar dynamical hardening flattens the spectrum ($\beta < 8/3$).

**Low-redshift sensitivity:** The redshift factor in $\langle h^4\rangle \propto \int dz\,p_z(z)(1+z)/\chi^2(z)$
diverges as $z\to 0$ for the LM ($\gamma=1$) and SZQ ($\gamma=0.5$) redshift
distributions. Results depend on an imposed $z_{\rm min}=0.05$ cutoff.

**LSS contribution** (their Eqs. 24–26): Standard Limber-approximation projection,
$C_{\ell,h^2}^{\rm LSS} \propto b_{\rm BH}^2$, frequency-independent (fractional
fluctuations cancel the frequency dependence). Even for $\langle b_{\rm BH}\rangle=5$
and SZQ, the LSS is at least 100$\times$ below shot noise at $f=0.1\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$.

## Their detection claim

From the abstract (this is the quote verified in `discussion.tex:45–47`):

> "near-future PTA data $\ldots$ should be capable of detecting this signal or
> placing meaningful constraints on SMBHB merger models."

More precisely (conclusions, p. 7): predicted shot-noise $C_\ell$ values "are
typically comparable to, or exceed, the current broad frequency-band NANOGrav
constraints," and a two-parameter fit (scale-independent shot-noise amplitude
plus frequency power-law index) may facilitate detection or tight bounds.
They explicitly acknowledge that sample variance is large and that the full
probability distribution must be modeled—but this caveat is in their conclusions,
not their abstract claim.

## Relevance to the project's argument

Three specific gaps this paper has relative to the project's critique:

1. **Mean vs. median.** The paper computes $\mathbb E[C_\ell^{\rm SN}] = 4\pi/N_{\rm eff}$,
   the ensemble mean. For heavy-tailed source distributions (low $N_{\rm eff}$), the
   median lies far below the mean. The paper acknowledges "large sample variance"
   and "skewness" in conclusions, but the abstract and headline comparison to NANOGrav
   bounds uses the mean. This overstates the typical realization.

2. **$p^4$ scaling of $C_\ell$ sensitivity.** The project's hierarchy shows
   $D_{C_\ell} \propto p^4$, while coherent source fitting gives $D_{\rm coh} \propto p$.
   Lin et al. do not compare $C_\ell$ against coherent strategies; their entire analysis
   is within the $C_\ell$ framework.

3. **NANOGrav bounds are uninformative (prior-dominated).** The paper's Fig. 1
   comparison treats the NANOGrav $3\sigma$ upper bounds as genuine constraints.
   The project shows (Sec.~\ref{sec:nanograv}) these bounds are the sqrt-SH prior
   and carry no astrophysical information, so the "comparable to bounds" framing is
   vacuous.

## Caveats / open questions

- The paper uses $N_{\rm eff} \equiv \langle h^2\rangle^2 / \langle h^4\rangle$ (a
  continuum/ensemble version), which differs from the project's discrete definition
  $N_{\rm eff} = 1/\sum_a p_a^2$. In the many-source limit these coincide, but for
  a finite realized population they differ. The project's framework is the realized
  (single-draw) quantity; Lin et al. work with its ensemble average.
- The $z_{\rm min}$ sensitivity is a real theoretical uncertainty acknowledged by the
  authors but not propagated into their headline comparison with NANOGrav bounds.
- No treatment of the pulsar-response transfer function ($C_\ell^{(b)}$ vs
  $C_\ell^{(P)}$); their $C_{\ell,h^2}$ is a source-sky quantity (analogous to the
  project's $C_\ell^{(P)}$), consistent with project conventions.
