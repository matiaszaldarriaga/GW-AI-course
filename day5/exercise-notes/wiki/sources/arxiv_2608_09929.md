**Authors:** Meng-Xiang Lin, Adam Lidz, Chung-Pei Ma — same three authors as [[arxiv_2602_16808]]
**Submitted:** 2026-08-10; astro-ph.CO; 12 pages, 6 figures; no journal ref yet
**Bib key:** `LinLidzMa:2026b` (provisional; NOT yet in `paper_v2/references.bib`)

## Summary

Lin, Lidz & Ma follow up their analytic shot-noise paper ([[arxiv_2602_16808]],
hereafter LLM26a) by replacing the ensemble-mean estimate with Monte Carlo
realization PDFs. They Poisson-sample the SMBHB merger distribution in
$(M_{\rm BH}, q, z)$ cells and form, per realization,
$\hat h_c^2 = \sum_i N_i h_i^2$, $\hat h_c^4 = \sum_i N_i h_i^4$, and
$\hat C_{\rm shot}/(4\pi) = \hat h_c^4/(\hat h_c^2)^2$; $7\times10^5$
realizations per frequency, fiducial population LM24 (Liepold & Ma), alternate
SZQ (Sato-Polito, Zaldarriaga & Quataert). Headline: the shot-noise PDF is
broad (95% interval $\simeq 1.7$ dex) and positively skewed; median and mode
lie a factor $\sim2$–$3$ below the ensemble mean; and the ensemble mean itself
departs from the moment-based estimate $\langle h^4\rangle/\langle h^2\rangle^2$
of LLM26a by a factor $\sim3$ at $f = 0.1\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$, growing to $>2$
orders of magnitude at $f = 1\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$, because $\langle X/Y\rangle \neq
\langle X\rangle/\langle Y\rangle$. They also compute PDFs conditioned on a
noisy $\hat h_c^2$ measurement, and test $z_{\rm min}$, $M_{\rm BH,max}$ and a
stellar-hardening residence-time variant.

## Key equations

- **Per-realization shot noise** (their Eq. 10, §II B):
  $$\frac{\hat C_{\rm shot}}{4\pi} = \frac{\hat h_c^4}{(\hat h_c^2)^2}
    = \frac{\sum_i N_i h_i^4}{\left[\sum_i N_i h_i^2\right]^2}.$$
  With $w_i \equiv h_i^2/\sum_j N_j h_j^2$ this is $\sum_i N_i w_i^2$, i.e.
  **exactly this project's ledger $\sum_a p_a^2 = 1/N_{\rm eff}$** — see
  "Consistency notes" below. Their own reading (§II B, after Eq. 15) is the
  same: "it is natural to interpret shot-noise as the inverse of the effective
  number of sources $\hat C_{\rm shot}/(4\pi) \equiv 1/N_{\rm eff}$".
- **Bounds** (§II B, Eqs. 12–15):
  $$\frac{1}{N} \le \frac{\hat C_{\rm shot}}{4\pi} \le 1,$$
  upper equality iff a single source carries all the power; lower equality iff
  all sources are equally bright. (Both are the standard bounds on an inverse
  participation ratio; the proofs in the paper are correct.)
- **NANOGrav dictionary** (§II A): $\hat C_{\rm shot}/(4\pi)$ equals NANOGrav's
  $\tilde C_{\ell>0}/\tilde C_{\ell=0}$ — same $1/4\pi$ relation as in LLM26a.
- **Poisson sampling** (Eqs. 8–9): $N_i \sim \mathcal P(\overline{\Delta N_i})$
  with $\overline{\Delta N_i} = \frac{d^4N}{dM\,dq\,dz\,d\ln f}\Delta M\Delta q\Delta z$,
  **per $d\ln f$, not per PTA frequency bin** (their §II B, explicit).
- **Conditional PDF** (Eq. 16): $P(y\mid\tilde x) = \int dx\,P(y\mid x)P_{\rm noise}(x\mid\tilde x)$
  with $x=\log_{10}\hat h_c^2$, $y = \log_{10}\hat h_c^4$ or $\log_{10}[\hat C_{\rm shot}/4\pi]$.
- **Stellar-hardening residence time** (Eq. 17):
  $\left.\frac{dt_r}{d\ln f_r}\right|_{\rm tot} = \left.\frac{dt_r}{d\ln f_r}\right|_{\rm GW}
  \left[1+(f_b/f_r)^\kappa\right]^{-1}$, $\kappa = 10/3$, $f_b = 5$ nHz,
  mass-independent.

## What changed relative to LLM26a ([[arxiv_2602_16808]])

This is the most important content of this page. Five changes:

1. **The headline estimator of LLM26a is now stated to be wrong in the regime
   of interest.** LLM26a's Eq. 9, $C^{\rm SN}_{\ell>0,h^2} = \langle h^4\rangle/\langle h^2\rangle^2
   = 4\pi/N_{\rm eff}$, is here called "the naive analytic expectation" and shown
   to exceed the ensemble mean by 2.8× at $0.1\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$ and 130× at
   $1\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$ (§III A).
2. **The $f^{8/3}$ frequency scaling of LLM26a Eq. 12 is superseded** by
   $f^{1.1-1.2}$ for the mean and median of the realized statistic (§III A).
3. **A hard ceiling $\hat C_{\rm shot}/(4\pi)\le1$ replaces an unbounded
   estimate.** They say so explicitly: "whereas $\langle\hat h^4_c\rangle/
   \langle\hat h^2_c\rangle^2$ can be arbitrarily large [LLM26a],
   $\hat C_{\rm shot}/4\pi$ ... is strictly bounded above by unity."
4. **The $N_{\rm eff}$ definition moves from ensemble to realized.** LLM26a used
   $N_{\rm eff} = \langle h^2\rangle^2/\langle h^4\rangle$; here it is
   $1/\sum_a p_a^2$ in a single draw. This is the definitional switch already
   flagged as a caveat in [[arxiv_2602_16808]] ("Caveats", first bullet) and in
   [[../concepts/shot_noise]].
5. **The detection claim is materially softened.** LLM26a: predicted $C_\ell$
   "typically comparable to, or exceed, the current broad frequency-band
   NANOGrav constraints", and near-future data "should be capable of detecting
   this signal or placing meaningful constraints". Here the median prediction is
   $67\times$ below the NANOGrav bound at 3 nHz and $4\times$ below at 30 nHz,
   and the abstract's claim is reduced to "shot-noise nevertheless provides a
   powerful diagnostic ... interpreting PTA measurements, however, requires
   modeling its full probability distribution."

**Not changed:** they still compare against the NANOGrav 15-yr $C_\ell$ upper
limit as if it were a measurement. The only new hedge is one sentence — "The
current NANOGrav bound is also sensitive to the analysis priors adopted" (§III A)
— with no analysis of the sqrt-SH prior. Critique #3 of
[[arxiv_2602_16808]] therefore stands untouched, and critique #2 of the
$p/p^2/p^4$ detection hierarchy is still not engaged: the paper works entirely
in the $C_\ell$/moment framework.

## New material with no counterpart in our draft

- **Conditioning on $\hat h_c^2$** (§III B, their Fig. 3): the conditional
  $P(\hat C_{\rm shot}\mid\tilde h_c^2)$ narrows from 1.5–3 dex to 0.7–0.8 dex
  once $\sigma_{\log_{10}h_c^2}\lesssim0.1$ ($f=0.1/{\rm yr}$) or $\lesssim0.2$–$0.3$
  ($f=1/{\rm yr}$). This is an independent statement of our Discussion's ranking
  (the spectrum is the most informative observable available now).
- **Stellar-hardening residence times** (§IV B): $h_c^2$ and $h_c^4$ are strong
  discriminators between GW-driven and hardening models, but $\hat C_{\rm shot}$
  is *not*, because the suppression partly cancels in the ratio. A ratio
  statistic loses model information the numerator carries.
- **$z_{\rm min}$ insensitivity of the median** (§IV A, their Fig. 5): mean
  $h_c^4$ is sensitive to $z_{\rm min}\in[0.005,0.2]$; the median and the
  shot-noise are not. Answers an open question flagged in [[arxiv_2602_16808]]
  ("Caveats", second bullet).
- **A pointer toward the source-based search**: "In the single source limit,
  the pulsar-pair correlations in an individual realization follow a well-defined
  overlap reduction function, distinct from the Hellings-Downs curve
  [Mingarelli et al. 2026, arXiv:2603.05722]. This can be used to complement
  traditional anisotropy searches at high frequencies." (§II B, final paragraph.)
  Directionally our argument, credited to a different paper.

## Consistency notes (REFINES / EXTENDS / CONTRADICTS)

- **REFINES [[../concepts/shot_noise]].** The page's "$N_{\rm eff}$ definition
  subtlety" caveat is now resolved in our favour: LLM adopt the realized
  $1/\sum_a p_a^2$. The page's "What this project adds beyond the mean" section
  must be rewritten — LLM now give the PDF too. What our work still adds beyond
  theirs is the *angular* realization (see next bullet) and the detection-theory
  ranking.
- **EXTENDS [[../concepts/dipole_distribution]] — the one substantive
  methodological gap.** LLM26b evaluates $\hat C_{\rm shot}$ "directly from
  realizations of the sky-averaged moments, rather than constructing full
  angular maps ... because the shot-noise is $\ell$-independent" (§II B). Our
  realizations place every source on the sphere, so this is testable. Result:
  their $\ell$-independence claim is **verified** — the ensemble mean of the
  realized $C_\ell/C_0$ is flat over $\ell=1$–$6$ and equals $\sum_a p_a^2$ to
  the third digit in all three models. But $\sum_a p_a^2$ is
  $\mathbb E[C_\ell/C_0\mid\{p_a\}]$, *not* the realized $C_\ell/C_0$: the ratio
  $(C_1/C_0)/\sum_a p_a^2$ has a 95% range of 1.15–1.41 dex on top of their
  1.7 dex. Averaging over multipoles removes most of it (0.41–0.52 dex for
  $\ell\le3$, 0.22–0.28 dex for $\ell\le6$), so their PDF is adequate for a
  broadband amplitude such as NANOGrav's $\tilde C_{\ell>0}/\tilde C_0$ but
  understates the spread of any single-multipole measurement.
- **EXTENDS — binning convention.** Their $\overline{\Delta N}$ is per
  $d\ln f$; ours is per PTA bin, $\Delta\ln f = 1/(f T_{\rm obs})$, which at
  $f = 1\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$, $T_{\rm obs} = 16.03$ yr is $1/16$ of a decade in
  $\ln f$. A PTA sky map at a given Fourier bin is built from the sources in
  that bin, so the per-bin population is the observationally relevant one and
  carries **fewer** sources, hence **more** shot noise, at high frequency. They
  flag this as future work in their Conclusions ("one should average the
  differential quantities considered here, defined per $d\ln f$, over the
  finite frequency bins"). Direction of the correction is known (upward at high
  $f$); the magnitude is not computed here.
- **Does NOT contradict [[../concepts/fisher_hierarchy]] or
  [[../concepts/matched_filter_vs_power]]** — the detection-theory content of
  our paper is simply absent from theirs.
- **Model-normalization caveat.** Our three models are matched to
  $\sqrt{\langle h_c^2\rangle} = 2.5\times10^{-15}$ at $f=1\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$ by
  rescaling $\phi_\sigma$ by 13.3, 6.1 and 0.72; the low-scatter models
  therefore contain $\sim13\times$ more black holes than observed and are
  correspondingly *less* shot-noise-dominated. LM24 is not rescaled. This
  accounts for most of the $\sim5\times$ gap between their LM24 median and our
  $\varepsilon=0.20$ median at 3 nHz, and is why LM24 lands between our two
  low-scatter models rather than on one of them.
