# The distribution of the gravitational-wave background from supermassive black holes

**Source:** `references/2406.17010/` (arxiv 2406.17010)
**Bib key:** `SatoPolito:2025dist` — see [[../bibliography]]
**Type:** arxiv paper (the precursor / foundation of this project)
**Ingested:** 2026-04-13

## Summary

Sato-Polito & Zaldarriaga (PRD 111 023043, 2025) develop an analytic
characteristic-function framework for the full probability distribution
$p(h_c^2(f)|\vec\theta)$ of the GWB produced by a discrete SMBHB
population. The key move is to cast the problem in terms of a GW
luminosity function $dN/dh_s^2$ and use the confusion-limit / Fourier
(characteristic-function) method to sum independent Poisson-drawn
sources. Applied to the NANOGrav 15-yr free spectrum, the analysis
confirms a factor 5-12 excess in required $\rho_{\rm BH}$ relative to
local scaling relations and adds a new constraint: the spectrum shape
disfavors mass functions dominated by a small number of very massive
sources, requiring $M_{\rm peak} \lesssim 10^{10}\,M_\odot$ at
$2\sigma$.

## Key results

- **BH density discrepancy.** Fitting the NANOGrav 15-yr free spectrum
  (HD-w/MP+DP+CURN, 14 bins) requires $\rho_{\rm BH} \approx 5$-$12
  \times \rho_{\rm BH,\,fid}$ (fiducial $4.5\times10^5\,M_\odot\,{\rm
  Mpc}^{-3}$), consistent with `SatoPolito:2023big` but now using full
  spectral shape.
- **Peak mass constraint.** $M_{\rm peak} \lesssim 10^{10}\,M_\odot$
  at $2\sigma$. The spectral shape breaks the amplitude degeneracy along
  lines of constant $N_c$.
- **Individual sources.** Maximum detectable-source count consistent
  with the isotropic background is $\bar N_{\rm tot} \lesssim 0.1$
  (redshift-optimized upper bound); fiducial model gives $\bar N \sim
  10^{-3}$. An order-of-magnitude sensitivity improvement is needed for
  $\bar N \sim 1$.
- **Fiducial scatter.** $\varepsilon_0 = 0.38$ (McConnell & Ma
  $M$-$\sigma$); VDF from Bernardi et al. 2010 with
  $\phi_* = 2.61\times10^{-2}\,{\rm Mpc}^{-3}$, $\sigma_* = 159.6\,{\rm
  km/s}$, $\alpha=0.41$, $\beta=2.59$. These are the exact values used
  in the present project.
- **Multiple-merger upper limit.** Equal-mass mergers with $\tau=1\,{\rm
  Gyr}$ and no accretion boost $\phi_\star\to 2.67\,\phi_\star$ and
  lower $M_{\rm peak}\to 0.43\,M_{\rm peak}$; still discrepant.

## Distribution framework

**Characteristic-function approach.** The distribution of the aggregate
power $h_t^2 = \sum_s h_s^2$ is computed by exponentiating the
single-source Fourier transform:
$$\hat P(\omega) = \exp\!\left[\int dh_s^2\,\frac{dN}{dh_s^2}
  \bigl(e^{i\omega h_s^2}-1\bigr)\right].$$
This is exact for Poisson-distributed independent sources; it replaces
Monte Carlo with a direct numerical FFT.

**Gaussian-core + power-law-tail decomposition.** For a power-law LF
$dN/dh_s^2 \propto (h_s^2)^{-\gamma}$ with $1<\gamma<3$:
- Core: set by the numerous faint sources ($h_s^2 < h_{s,1}^2$ where
  $N(>h_{s,1}^2)=1$); approximated by a Gaussian with
  $\sigma^2_{\rm peak}\approx\int_0^{x_1}(dN/dx)x^2\,dx$.
- Tail: $P(h_t^2)\approx dN/dh_s^2$ for $h_t^2 \gg h_{s,1}^2$;
  dominated by the probability of one bright source of strain
  $\sim h_t^2$.
- Peak of distribution: $h_{c,\rm peak}^2 = \int_0^{h_{s,1}^2}
  (dN/dh_s^2)\,h_s^2\,dh_s^2$ (equation 4 / eq:h2peak in the paper).

**Scaled universal function $\mu(x)$.** Defining $x=h_s^2/h_s^2|_{\rm
peak}$, the LF is $dN/d\log x = N_c\,\mu(x)$ where $\mu$ is
approximately universal (weakly dependent on $M_{\rm peak}$, frequency).
The mean satisfies $\bar x_t = N_c$. The full parameter dependence
reduces to scanning over $N_c$: this is the direct antecedent of this
project's $p$ (brightest-source fraction) as the single control
parameter.

**Reusable pieces for the anisotropy extension.** (1) The LF
$dN/d\log h_s^2$ is the same object needed to compute the source-power
sky $C_\ell^{(P)}$ — just insert angular positions. (2) The
Gaussian-core / power-law-tail split maps onto the coherent vs.
incoherent decomposition in the present project: the core corresponds
to the many-source background; the tail to the one-source-dominated
regime. (3) $N_c$ is a monotone function of $N_{\rm eff}$ and $p$.

## Relevance to the project's argument

This paper establishes the single-frequency distribution
$p(h_c^2(f)|\vec\theta)$; the present project extends the framework to
the joint distribution across sky positions, i.e., to
$p(C_\ell^{(P)}|\vec\theta)$. The KL hierarchy argument
(coherent $\propto p$ > incoherent covariance $\propto p^2$ >
$C_\ell$-compression $\propto p^4$) is a direct consequence of the
Gaussian-core / bright-tail structure: the tail (rare bright source)
drives the anisotropy signal and is best captured by a matched filter,
not by $C_\ell$ compression.

## Caveats / open questions

- The paper quotes $N_c$ numerically (eq. 6 in the paper) for fiducial
  parameters but does not give explicit median/mean strain ratios as
  a function of $\varepsilon$. The ratios cited in
  `wiki/concepts/SMBH_population_model.md` (e.g., 57% at
  $\varepsilon=0.66$) are attributed to this paper but do not appear
  verbatim in the tex; they may come from the figures or from the
  companion `SatoPolito:2023big`. Flag for verification before citing
  as a direct numerical claim.
- The characteristic-function FFT uses a finite grid
  ($x_{\rm min}=h_p^2/h_s^2|_{\rm peak}$, grid size $2^{24}$); boundary
  artifacts visible in Fig. 5 (Ggamma.pdf). Any reuse in this project
  should validate the grid independently.
- The paper fits the first 14 frequency bins; the present project
  extends the analysis to angular structure, so the frequency-bin
  independence assumption (noted as verified in `Lamb_freespec`) should
  be checked when combining spatial and spectral data.
